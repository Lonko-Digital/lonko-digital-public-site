How Much Do Facebook Ads Cost? Facebook and Instagram Ad Costs in 2026

13 min read

Last updated: October 28, 2026

How much do Facebook ads cost? For US advertisers, the typical median is about $0.70 per click for traffic campaigns, $1.92 per click for lead campaigns, and $27.66 per lead (WordStream by LocaliQ, 2025 benchmarks). Meta's average cost per 1,000 impressions was $8.32 across 2025 and rose above $9.70 in November (Gupta Media).

Those numbers are a starting point, not a quote. On Meta, social media advertising cost is set in an auction, not by a rate card. You set your own budget on Meta, and what you actually pay depends on who you target, what you ask Meta to optimize for, how good your ads are, and when you run them. This guide explains how Meta pricing works, what Facebook and Instagram ads cost right now by industry, why prices climb into Black Friday, and how a small business can set a starting budget and tell whether the money is working.

How Facebook and Instagram ad pricing works

Meta sells ad space through an auction. According to Meta's own explanation of its ad auction, every time there is a chance to show someone an ad, an auction decides which ad appears, and unlike a traditional auction the highest bid does not automatically win. Meta weighs your bid against how relevant and useful it expects your ad to be for that person. A well-targeted, engaging ad can win placements for less than a weaker ad with a bigger bid.

You do not pay a fixed price. Meta's help page on ad costs puts it simply: when you advertise on Meta, you decide how much you want to spend. You control spend with a daily or lifetime budget, a campaign spending limit, and an account spending limit.

CPM, CPC and cost per result

Three numbers describe what you pay:

  • CPM (cost per 1,000 impressions): what it costs for your ad to be shown 1,000 times. This is the base price of attention on the platform.
  • CPC (cost per click): your total spend divided by the number of clicks. It depends on CPM and on how often people click (click-through rate).
  • Cost per result: your spend divided by the outcome you care about, such as a lead, a purchase or a booked call. For a business owner this is the number that matters most.

What you are actually charged for depends on your campaign objective. Meta's help page on getting charged explains that, depending on the objective, you may be charged for impressions, link clicks or other events. One detail many new advertisers miss: a new ad account must spend at least $10 USD charged on impressions before it can switch to another charge type (Meta Business Help Center, accessed October 2026).

Instagram ads cost vs Facebook ads cost

Facebook and Instagram ads are bought in the same place, Meta Ads Manager, through the same auction. Instagram is a set of placements (Feed, Stories, Reels, Explore) rather than a separate ad platform. That is why many benchmark reports, including Gupta Media's CPM tracker, give one combined Meta figure.

Where the two have been separated, the trend has favored Facebook on price. Tinuiti's Q4 2025 Digital Ads Benchmark Report found that Facebook CPM fell 13% year over year in the fourth quarter of 2025, while Instagram CPM rose 8%. The same report notes that Instagram CPMs have risen sharply over the past couple of years while Facebook CPMs have been flat to down, and that Reels made up 29% of Instagram ad impressions in Q4 2025.

So how much do Instagram ads cost compared with Facebook? Per impression, recently Instagram has often been more expensive. But a cheaper impression is not automatically a cheaper customer. If your buyers spend their time on Instagram, a higher CPM there can still produce a lower cost per result. The practical approach is to let Meta test placements and then check your results by placement in Ads Manager before cutting one.

What drives the cost of Facebook ads

Five things move your price more than anything else:

  1. Audience. Narrow, high-value or heavily targeted audiences have more advertisers competing for them, which raises CPM.
  2. Objective. Asking Meta for purchases or leads usually costs more per impression than asking for reach or clicks, because Meta has to find people more likely to act.
  3. Creative quality. Because the auction rewards relevance, an ad people engage with can win placements for less. Tired ads that people scroll past get more expensive over time.
  4. Budget and duration. Meta's ad auction page lists sufficient budget and enough duration among the components of a successful campaign and recommends running with a sufficient budget for at least 7 days so delivery can stabilize.
  5. Season. Competition for attention changes through the year, and it peaks in the holiday shopping season.

Why Facebook ads get more expensive in November and around Black Friday

From October to December, retailers pour budget into Meta to reach holiday shoppers. The number of people scrolling does not grow nearly as fast as the number of advertisers bidding for them, so auction prices rise.

Gupta Media's CPM tracker shows the pattern clearly for 2025:

Month (2025) Average Meta CPM
October $6.95
November $9.72
December $10.02
Full-year 2025 average $8.32

By our calculation from those figures, November's average CPM was about 40% higher than October's, and December's was about 20% above the full-year average. The peak days are higher still: Gupta Media reported an average Meta CPM of $16.85 on Black Friday 2024 and $17.70 on Cyber Monday 2024, roughly double a typical month.

Not every quarter follows the same curve. Tinuiti found that Meta's overall CPM in Q4 2025 was actually 7% lower than in Q4 2024, as impressions grew 17% year over year. The seasonal rise within a year is reliable; the size of it changes from year to year.

What this means for a small business that is not a retailer: you are still in the same auction. If you sell services and do not need holiday shoppers, late November is often a good time to lower spend, protect your best-performing ads, and push harder in January when retail budgets pull back. If you do sell gifts or products, plan your budget and creative in October rather than reacting in the week of Black Friday.

One more thing to check this season: beginning October 28, 2026, Meta turns on Product Browsing by default for many existing ads, which can send people to an in-app page before your website. Our Chronicle explains what changes and how to check your ads.

Facebook ads minimum daily budget

Meta does not publish one fixed minimum daily budget for every advertiser. Its help page on minimum budgets says minimums vary by location and by how you are charged, and Ads Manager will alert you if your budget is below the minimum. Some advanced ad types have their own minimum spend requirements.

Meta does give one useful rule of thumb. If you use the cost per result goal bid strategy, Meta recommends a daily budget of at least five times your goal. For example, with a $5 goal, Meta suggests at least $25 per day (Meta Business Help Center, Best practices for minimum budgets, accessed October 2026).

In practice, the technical minimum is a few dollars a day. The useful minimum is whatever lets Meta collect enough results to learn, which is a function of your cost per result.

Facebook ads for small business: how to set a starting budget

Work backward from the result you want rather than picking a round number.

  1. Choose one result to pay for. A lead, a booked call or a sale. One campaign, one goal.
  2. Estimate your cost per result. Use your own past data if you have it. If not, start with your industry's benchmark from the table above.
  3. Decide how many results you need to judge the test. A handful of leads tells you very little. Twenty or more gives you something to read.
  4. Multiply. Results needed times expected cost per result gives your test budget. Spread it over at least 7 days, in line with Meta's own guidance.
  5. Check it against what a customer is worth. If the math says you would pay more for a customer than that customer brings in, fix the offer or the targeting before you spend.

Illustrative example (not a benchmark): a home improvement business aiming for 20 leads uses the industry median cost per lead of $41.26. That suggests a test budget of about $825, or roughly $27 per day over a month. If 1 in 5 leads becomes a job, the business is paying about $206 per customer from ads alone before counting its own time. That figure only makes sense if an average job is worth well more than $206.

This is why "how much do Facebook ads cost" has a second half: compared to what? The answer is your customer acquisition cost and what a customer is worth to you over time.

How to tell if your Facebook ad spend is working

Meta's dashboard will show you plenty of numbers. These are the ones that tell you whether the money is earning its keep:

  • Cost per result against your target. Decide in advance what you can afford to pay for a lead or a sale, and compare every campaign to that number.
  • Result quality, not just volume. Cheap leads that never answer the phone are expensive. Track which ad each lead came from and whether it became a customer.
  • Your own records, not only Meta's. Meta's reporting is a useful view, but your CRM, booking system or sales records are the final word on what turned into revenue.
  • Frequency and creative fatigue. When the same people see the same ad too often, results usually slip. Rising cost per result with steady targeting is often a creative problem.
  • Seasonal context. A higher CPM in November is expected. Judge it against last November, not against October.

If cost per result stays above your target after a fair test, change one thing at a time: the offer, the ad, the audience or the landing page.

FAQ

How much do Facebook ads cost per day?

You choose your daily budget, and Meta does not set one fixed minimum for everyone; minimums vary by location and charge type. A practical starting point is your expected cost per result times the number of results you need, spread over at least 7 days. If you use a cost per result goal, Meta recommends a daily budget of at least five times that goal.

Are Instagram ads more expensive than Facebook ads?

Per impression, they often have been recently. Tinuiti's Q4 2025 report found Instagram CPM up 8% year over year while Facebook CPM fell 13%. Both are bought in Meta Ads Manager through the same auction, so the better test is cost per result by placement for your own campaigns.

What is a good CPM for Facebook ads?

There is no universal good CPM. As a reference, Gupta Media's tracker put Meta's average CPM at $8.32 for 2025, with November at $9.72 and December at $10.02. CPM is the price of attention; what matters more is whether that attention turns into results at a cost you can afford.

Can a small business advertise on Facebook with a small budget?

Yes. Meta lets you set your own budget, and the technical minimum is low. The limit is learning: a very small budget produces few results, which makes it hard to know what is working. Start with one clear goal and a budget large enough to get at least a couple of dozen results.

Why are my Facebook ads more expensive in November?

Retailers increase spend for holiday shopping, so more advertisers compete for the same people. In 2025, Meta's average CPM rose from $6.95 in October to $9.72 in November (Gupta Media), and Black Friday and Cyber Monday 2024 averaged $16.85 and $17.70. If you do not sell holiday products, consider spending less in late November and more in January.
