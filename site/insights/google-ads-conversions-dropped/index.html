Your Google Ads Conversions Dropped. What to Check Before You Change Anything

Pillar: Understanding Your Numbers·October 28, 2026·4 min read

In short

A sudden drop in reported conversions has a few possible explanations, and only some of them mean the campaign got worse. Before you change bids or budget, check whether recent conversions just haven't been counted yet, whether tracking still works, what changed in the account, and whether real inquiries actually fell.

The last few weeks looked fine. Then you open Google Ads and conversions are down by half. The natural reaction is to fix something: raise bids, pause a campaign, rewrite the ads.

Hold off for now. A drop in the report and a drop in the business are not the same thing, and the first few checks take minutes.

Are the conversions late, not missing?

By default, Google Ads credits a conversion to the date of the click, not the date the person got in touch. Google says conversions can be reported up to 90 days after the click. So the most recent days in any report are often incomplete, and they fill in as late conversions arrive.

That is why comparing the last seven days with the seven days before can look alarming when nothing has changed. Cost is counted right away. Conversions trickle in. The recent period looks worse until it catches up.

If your customers usually take a few days to call or fill in a form, compare periods that ended at least that long ago. Where it is available, the Explanations feature in Google Ads can also flag conversion delay as the reason a number moved.

A drop in the report and a drop in the business are not the same thing.

Is your Google Ads conversion tracking still working?

If tracking breaks, conversions fall to near zero almost overnight while clicks stay steady. That pattern points to measurement, not performance.

In Google Ads, the list of conversion actions shows a status for each one. Check it, then submit a test form or make a test call and see whether it appears. Recent website changes are worth checking first: a new form, a moved thank-you page, or an update that removed the tag.

What changed in the account?

Google Ads keeps a change history of edits to campaigns, ad groups, keywords and budgets, with the date and who made each one. Open it and read the days just before the drop.

Look for a lower budget, a paused keyword or ad, a new location or audience setting, or a change to the bid strategy. Changes made by automated systems can appear there too. If you use an automated bid strategy, changing its settings or adding and removing campaigns can put it back into a learning period. Google says this typically takes one or two conversion cycles, so a dip right after a change may settle on its own.

Did real inquiries actually fall?

The last check happens outside Google Ads. Look at your inbox, phone log, booking system or CRM for the same dates, and count the inquiries you actually received.

If real inquiries held steady, the problem is in the reporting, and that is where the fix belongs. If they fell along with the report, the drop is real, and the questions shift to demand: a seasonal dip, a competitor, a pricing change, or something on your own site.

When to act

Match the response to what you found. A gap caused by conversion delay needs patience, not changes. Broken tracking needs a repair before you judge anything else. A recent account change can be reviewed and, if needed, reversed. A real drop in inquiries deserves a closer look, measured over a few weeks rather than a few days.

The short version

Before you fix a drop, confirm it. Check for late conversions, test your tracking, read the change history, and compare the report with the inquiries you actually received.
