AEO vs GEO vs SEO: What Generative Engine Optimization Means for Your Business

11 min read

Last updated: November 5, 2026

Generative engine optimization (GEO) means making your content easy for AI tools like ChatGPT and Google's AI Overviews to find, trust and cite. Answer engine optimization (AEO) means writing direct answers to the questions people ask. Search engine optimization (SEO) means earning visibility in search results. They overlap heavily, and good SEO is the foundation for all three.

That last point matters more than the new acronyms. Google's own guide to its AI features says SEO best practices "continue to be relevant" because those features are "rooted in our core Search ranking and quality systems." This guide defines each term in plain language, explains how AI tools choose which sources to cite, and lists the practical steps a business can take, including what you can safely ignore.

What is SEO?

Search engine optimization is the work of making your website easy for search engines to crawl, understand and rank, so the right people find it when they search. It covers the technical basics (pages that load, can be crawled and are indexed), content that answers what people search for, and signals of trust such as links from other reputable sites.

SEO is not going away. When an AI tool searches the web before answering, it still has to find pages, and it finds them through search indexes.

What is answer engine optimization (AEO)?

Answer engine optimization is the practice of structuring content so that it directly answers specific questions, in a form that search features and AI assistants can lift and quote. In practice it means a clear question as a heading, a short direct answer right below it, and supporting detail after that.

AEO grew out of featured snippets and voice search, where a single answer is read or shown to the user. The same habits help with AI answers today.

What is generative engine optimization (GEO)?

Generative engine optimization is the practice of improving how often and how accurately your content is used and cited in answers written by generative AI tools, such as ChatGPT, Google's AI Overviews and AI Mode, Perplexity and Copilot.

The term comes from a research paper, "GEO: Generative Engine Optimization," by researchers including Pranjal Aggarwal and Karthik Narasimhan, first posted in November 2023 and accepted to the KDD 2024 conference. The authors built a benchmark of queries and found that content changes could boost visibility in generative engine responses "by up to 40%," with results that varied by subject area. That is a lab result on a research benchmark, not a promise for any one website.

The overlap is the point. AEO is a writing discipline inside SEO, and GEO extends both to AI answers. A business that does the basics well is already doing most of what GEO asks for.

How AI tools choose which sources to cite

Each AI tool works differently, but the ones that search the web follow a similar pattern: they run searches, read the pages they retrieve, write an answer, and link to some of the sources they used.

Google describes this directly. Its guide explains that its AI features use retrieval-augmented generation, also called grounding, relying on "our core Search ranking systems to retrieve relevant, up-to-date web pages from our Search index." It also uses "query fan-out," a set of related searches the model runs at the same time to gather more relevant results for one question.

Two practical consequences follow:

  • To be cited, you first have to be found. Google says a page must be indexed and eligible to be shown in Search with a snippet to appear in its generative AI features. ChatGPT works the same way through its own crawler: OpenAI's documentation says sites that block OAI-SearchBot "will not be shown in ChatGPT search answers," and recommends allowing it in your robots.txt file.
  • Fan-out rewards depth. Because the AI runs several related searches for one question, a page that covers a topic thoroughly, including the follow-up questions people tend to ask, has more chances to be retrieved.

Is SEO dead because of AI search?

No, but the payoff is changing. Pew Research Center analyzed the browsing of 900 US adults in March 2025. When a Google search showed an AI summary, users clicked a traditional search result in 8% of visits, compared with 15% when no summary appeared. They clicked a link inside the AI summary itself in just 1% of visits.

So visibility inside the answer matters more than it used to, and a click is harder to win. But the way into the answer still runs through search: being indexed, being relevant, and being the clearest and most trustworthy source on the topic.

How to get found in AI search: what a business can do

None of these steps is new. What is new is how much they matter.

  1. Make sure you can be crawled and indexed. Check that important pages are indexed in Google Search Console and Bing Webmaster Tools. Check your robots.txt file, and if you want to appear in ChatGPT search answers, do not block OAI-SearchBot.
  2. Lead with the answer. Start each page or section with a short, direct answer to the question it targets, then add detail. Use the question as the heading when it reads naturally.
  3. Publish content no one else has. Google's guide calls non-commodity content the factor that will "likely influence your website's presence in generative AI search in the long run more than any of the other suggestions." Your own process, your prices, real examples and specific guidance beat a generic summary of what is already online.
  4. Cite your sources and date your facts. Specific, sourced statements are easier for an AI tool to check and quote, and they help readers trust you.
  5. Keep your business information consistent and current. Your name, services, locations and claims should match across your website, your Google Business Profile and directory listings. AI tools draw on what they find, and outdated pages are part of what they may find.
  6. Earn mentions on trusted sites. Coverage in trade publications, partner sites and reputable directories gives search engines and AI tools more independent evidence about who you are. Earn these genuinely, not through paid or fake mentions.
  7. Use FAQ sections and structured data where they fit. A short FAQ answers the follow-up questions that fan-out searches look for. Structured data helps with rich results in Google, though it is not a ticket into AI answers (more on that below).

Does schema markup help with AI answers?

Not directly in Google. Google's guide says structured data is "not required for generative AI search" and that "there's no special schema.org markup you need to add." It still recommends structured data as part of an overall SEO strategy because it helps pages become eligible for rich results.

The honest summary: keep using accurate structured data that matches what is visible on the page, and do not expect it to buy a place in AI answers.

What is llms.txt, and does it matter?

llms.txt is a proposed standard: a plain text file at the root of a website that summarizes the site and points AI tools to its most useful pages. It has attracted a lot of attention.

Here is what is confirmed. Google's guide lists llms.txt files and other "special" markup among things you can ignore, saying you don't need new machine-readable or AI text files "to appear in Google Search (including its generative AI capabilities), as Google Search itself doesn't use them." Google's Chrome team added an experimental llms.txt check to its Lighthouse tool for AI agents that browse websites, but Search Engine Journal reported in May 2026 that this check does not affect search rankings or AI citations.

As of November 2026, we found no public statement from other major AI providers confirming that they use llms.txt to choose what to cite. Adding one is harmless and quick, but it should come after the basics, not instead of them.

How to measure AI visibility

You cannot see a ranking report for ChatGPT, so measurement takes a few angles:

  • Google Search Console. Google's guide points site owners to the Generative AI performance report in Search Console to see how content performs in generative AI features in Search and Discover.
  • Referral traffic in your analytics. Visits from AI tools often arrive with a recognizable referrer, such as chatgpt.com or perplexity.ai. Track them as their own segment.
  • Manual answer checks. Ask the AI tools your customers use the questions you want to be known for, and record whether your business appears, whether it is cited, and whether what they say is accurate. Repeat on a regular schedule, since answers change.
  • Leads, not just mentions. As with any channel, the final word is in your own records. Ask new customers how they found you, and treat a sudden change in numbers with care before reacting. FAQ

Is SEO dead because of AI search?

No. Google says SEO best practices remain relevant because its AI features are rooted in its core Search ranking and quality systems. What has changed is the payoff: Pew Research Center found that users clicked a search result in 8% of visits when an AI summary appeared, compared with 15% without one.

What is the difference between AEO and GEO?

Answer engine optimization is about writing clear, direct answers to specific questions so they can be quoted. Generative engine optimization is broader: it aims to make your content used and cited in answers written by AI tools. AEO is one of the main habits that supports GEO.

How do I get my business mentioned by ChatGPT?

Make sure your site can be crawled, including by OpenAI's OAI-SearchBot, which OpenAI says sites must allow to appear in ChatGPT search answers. Then publish clear, original content that answers your customers' questions, keep your business information consistent across the web, and earn genuine mentions on trusted sites.

Does schema markup help with AI answers?

Not directly in Google. Google says structured data is not required for its generative AI features and there is no special schema.org markup to add. It still helps with rich results, so use accurate structured data that matches what is visible on the page.

What is llms.txt?

llms.txt is a proposed text file that summarizes a website for AI tools. Google says Google Search does not use it, including for its AI features. As of November 2026, we found no public statement from other major AI providers confirming that they use llms.txt to choose what to cite. It is harmless to add, but it is not a substitute for the basics.
