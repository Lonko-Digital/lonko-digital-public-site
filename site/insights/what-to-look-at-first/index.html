What Should I Actually Look at First?

Pillar: Understanding Your Numbers·3 min read

In short

Dashboards show everything with equal visual weight because a general-purpose tool usually doesn't know what matters to your specific business. Identify the primary number that would change your decision, then check only the one or two supporting signals needed to make sure you're not reading it out of context — and set the rest aside for now.

Open almost any ad account or analytics dashboard, and you'll see the same thing: rows and rows of numbers, all displayed in roughly the same size, the same color, the same visual weight. Impressions. Clicks. CTR. CPC. Bounce rate. Session duration. Conversion rate. Twenty, thirty, sometimes forty metrics, all presented as if they matter equally.

They don't. But nothing on the screen tells you that.

Why everything looks equally important

It's not an accident, and it's not laziness on the platform's part. A general-purpose dashboard usually doesn't know which number matters most to your specific business — it doesn't know your goals, your margins, or what decision you're actually trying to make this week. So it does the only neutral thing it can: it shows everything, and lets you sort it out.

That neutrality feels helpful in theory. In practice, it just moves the hard part onto you. Instead of interpreting one number, you're now faced with the harder task of first deciding which of thirty numbers deserves your attention — before you've even started actually thinking about your business.

A simple filter that cuts through this

Here's a question that does more work than any dashboard feature: what decision am I actually about to make?

Not "what should I check today" — that's still dashboard-shaped thinking. The real question is closer to: am I deciding whether to increase this budget, pause this campaign, or leave it alone? Once that's actually named, a second question follows naturally: identify the primary number that would change that decision, then check only the one or two supporting signals needed to make sure you're not reading it out of context.

That's usually one primary metric and perhaps one or two supporting signals. Not thirty.

What to do with everything else

Nothing. At least not yet.

This isn't about ignoring your other metrics forever — it's about sequencing. Everything else can wait until the first decision is made. Trying to hold thirty equally-weighted numbers in your head while also trying to decide something is how a five-minute check turns into an hour of scrolling without a clear outcome.

A useful habit: before opening any dashboard, write down — even just mentally — the one decision you're trying to inform. Then look specifically for the number that answers it, plus a quick check on whether that number is being read in context. If something else catches your eye along the way, it's fine to note it, but resist the pull to fully investigate it until the first question is actually answered.

Why this matters more than it seems

The paralysis of "everything looks important" isn't a sign you don't understand your data. It's what happens when a general-purpose tool meets a specific business question it was never built to answer directly.

The dashboard did its job — it showed you the numbers. Deciding which one matters, and which ones provide the necessary context, was always going to be a separate step, and it's a step most tools quietly leave out.

Reducing that gap — helping the number that actually matters surface on its own, with the right context alongside it, instead of leaving it buried among thirty others — is the specific kind of problem we're building Lonko to help with.
