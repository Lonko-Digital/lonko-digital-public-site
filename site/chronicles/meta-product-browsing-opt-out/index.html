Your Ads Are About to Send Customers Somewhere New First. Here's What to Check Before October 28.

Lonko Digital · 2026-10-23 · Updated 2026-10-22 · 6 min read

Last updated October 22, 2026. We'll update this if Meta publishes an official page on the change.

Right now, when someone taps your Facebook or Instagram ad, they land on your website. Starting October 28, Meta Product Browsing changes that by default for a lot of advertisers already running ads.

What happened

Meta's notice to advertisers says it plainly: "Starting October 28, 2026, we'll add product browsing to your existing ads." Instead of going straight from an ad tap to your website, shoppers may first see an in-app page on Facebook or Instagram built from your product catalog, pricing, and images, plus personalized suggestions based on their own shopping behavior. They can still click through to your site from there, but the direct path is gone unless you opt out first.

The notice reportedly started circulating with advertisers around October 14, giving about two weeks before the default flips on October 28, which lands almost exactly 30 days before Black Friday.

What Meta says it does

Meta says its own 16-day experiment (August 6 to 21, 2026) across roughly 117,700 advertisers in e-commerce, CPG, and retail, showed 3% more conversions on average with Product Browsing turned on, at better than 99% statistical confidence. That's Meta's own reported claim, not an independently verified result; Meta hasn't published its methodology, so it's not clear how conversions were defined, how comparison groups were built, or what happened to cost per result or revenue, not just conversion count. A 3% average across tens of thousands of accounts also doesn't guarantee anything about your specific account, your catalog, or your audience.

A note on where this information is coming from

The primary source here is Meta's own notice to advertisers, quoted above. As of this writing, Meta hasn't published that notice as a public Help Center article, blog post, or newsroom announcement, so what's available is the notice itself, as reported by PPC Land and corroborated independently by other ad-industry outlets. Worth checking Meta's own Ads Manager and Help Center directly for your account before October 28, in case anything has been clarified or changed since this was written. We'll update this article if Meta publishes an official public page.

Why it matters, especially right now

Adding a step between the ad tap and your website changes your funnel, even if the end result is a sale. You lose a direct, trackable site visit in exchange for an in-app experience you don't fully control, including personalized product suggestions that advertisers have flagged as a risk if your own catalog is incomplete or your margins depend on exactly what a shopper sees first. And the timing is the part worth sitting with: this turns on 30 days before Black Friday, right as most small businesses are finalizing the exact campaigns this would apply to.

If your ads already point to a strong, fast, well-merchandised product catalog, this might genuinely help. If you're running lifestyle or brand-awareness ads without a catalog behind them, or your site experience is a meaningful part of why people buy from you, this is worth testing before you assume it's neutral or good.

How to opt out of Meta product browsing

  1. Use the "Opt out" button in Meta's notice to your account (advertisers describe receiving this by email), before October 28, 2026.
  2. Or turn it off per ad, anytime, in that ad's Advantage+ Creative and Destination settings.

You can change this setting at any time, before or after October 28, it isn't a one-time, irreversible choice. No bulk, account-wide opt-out has been confirmed publicly as of this writing, so if you manage several ad accounts or a lot of active ads, budget time to check more than one.

What to actually do before October 28

Audit your product catalog in Commerce Manager now: pricing, images, and descriptions all feed directly into what shoppers see in this experience. If you want real data instead of guessing, run a short side-by-side test first, Product Browsing on for part of your budget, off for the rest, using your own conversion and revenue numbers rather than Meta's reported average. And set a baseline now for how much traffic actually reaches your website from ads, so you can tell later whether this changed it.

What not to overreact to

This isn't Meta hiding a price increase or quietly taking money from you. It's a UX change to the click path, it's reversible per ad at any time, and if your catalog and offer are strong, the in-app experience might convert just as well or better. The 3% figure is also an average, not a guarantee, so the right response is to test it against your own numbers, not to assume it will help or hurt before you've checked.

What to watch next

Two things nobody can answer yet, including us: whether new ads created after October 28 also default to Product Browsing being on is unknown, since Meta's notice doesn't say either way, and whether Meta ever publishes an official page on this the way it usually does for changes this size. Until then, the in-app notice and your own test results are the most reliable things to go on.

The bigger lesson here

This is the same pattern as a platform changing what counts as a billable lead or auto-upgrading your campaign settings: something about how your ads work changes by default, and you only notice if you were paying attention that week. The habit worth building isn't checking every platform every day. It's knowing where each platform tends to post these changes, and setting a reminder to check before a date like this one.
