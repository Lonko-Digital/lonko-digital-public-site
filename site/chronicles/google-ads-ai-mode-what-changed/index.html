Your Google Ads Are Already Running Inside AI Mode. Here's What That Actually Means.

Lonko Digital · 2026-11-07 · 9 min read

Google confirmed on October 28 that ads are now showing up inside AI Mode, including in follow-up questions within the same conversation. If you run Search, Shopping, or Performance Max campaigns, you didn't need to do anything for this to start. You still don't need to do anything for it to keep happening.

That's worth five minutes of attention, not because it's an emergency, but because it's exactly the kind of change that's easy to miss until it shows up in your numbers with no explanation attached.

What actually happened

Google announced it is launching "multiturn" ads in AI Mode in the United States. In plain terms: AI Mode lets someone search, get an AI-generated answer, then ask a follow-up question in the same conversation. Until now, ads could appear alongside that first answer. Now they can also appear in the answers to the follow-up questions.

At the same time, Google said it's expanding ads in AI Mode to 10 more countries: Australia, Canada, India, Kenya, Malaysia, New Zealand, Nigeria, Pakistan, the Philippines, and Singapore, with Brazil, Mexico, and the United Kingdom planned later this quarter. Google's own Ads Help page lists a slightly different, shorter set of markets where ads in AI Mode are currently live, which tells you this is a rollout still actively expanding, not a single finished list.

Google also shared two figures worth knowing: AI Mode searches run about three times longer, on average, than a typical Google search, and in its own internal testing, click-through rates on sponsored product listings in AI Mode are already similar to organic ones. Google's own May 2026 AI Mode report adds another data point: follow-up questions in the US were already rising more than 40% a month on average even before this week's announcement, which is part of why Google is building an ad format specifically for them now.

This isn't a sudden move. Google has been testing ads inside AI Mode since at least October 2025, added Shopping ads there in February 2026 (citing more than 75 million daily active users at the time), and moved Shopping ads directly into the body of AI Mode's answers at the end of August 2026. This week's announcement is the next step in a rollout that's been building for a year, not a one-off experiment.

It also lands next to a number worth knowing if you want the full picture of why Google is pushing here. On the same day as this announcement, Alphabet reported Google Search & other revenue of $63.35 billion for the third quarter of 2026, up 12% year over year. That's barely more in dollar terms than the previous quarter's $63.27 billion, but growth slowed from 17% in the second quarter to 12% in the third, according to Alphabet's own earnings releases. None of that tells you anything about your own account. It does help explain why Google has an obvious business reason to keep expanding ad surfaces inside AI Mode rather than treating it as a side experiment.

AI Mode isn't AI Overviews, and the difference matters here

It's easy to confuse this with AI Overviews, the AI-generated summary that can appear at the top of a regular Google search. We covered what AI Overviews are doing to organic clicks in an earlier piece, and it's a genuinely different story.

AI Mode is a separate, more conversational search experience, built around back-and-forth questions rather than a single query. This new ad placement lives specifically inside that conversational experience, not inside the AI Overview box on a normal search results page. If you've been thinking about "AI in Google Search" as one thing, this is a good moment to split it into two: the AI Overview that can appear above your organic listing, and AI Mode, a different tab where a conversation happens and where ads can now follow that conversation. The two surfaces also differ in how AI search tools choose what to cite, which matters just as much for how your business shows up organically as it does for where these new ads appear.

Why your campaigns are already eligible, whether you asked or not

Here's the part that actually matters for your account: there's no signup for this. Google's own documentation is direct about it. Existing Search, Shopping, and Performance Max campaigns are automatically eligible for ads in AI Mode. No new campaign type, no separate budget line, no toggle to switch on.

There's also no way to opt out of AI Overviews or AI Mode specifically, and you can't target them exclusively either. The only tools you have to shape where your ads show are the ones you likely already use: negative keywords and brand exclusions.

A few targeting details worth knowing if you manage your own account: AI Max for Search and broad match keywords (on Search, Shopping, and Performance Max) are built to match against the meaning of the AI's response, not just your literal keyword. Exact and phrase match keywords are more conservative. They only match queries that show clear, direct commercial intent. If your account leans heavily on exact match for control, you're likely seeing less of this than an account built on broad match and Smart Bidding.

Worth knowing too: the responses themselves can include "AI explainers," written by Google's Gemini models and drawn partly from your own website content, that sit alongside the ads. You can't write, edit, or opt out of those explainers, so part of how your business gets described in AI Mode is now generated by Google, not by you.

Billing works the same way it already does. Same auction, same cost-per-click or conversion-based bidding as standard Search ads. There's no extra charge just for showing up in AI Mode, and you only pay for valid clicks.

What to actually check in your account this week

You don't need to rebuild anything. You do want to know what you're looking at:

  1. Check your search terms report for anything that reads like a longer, more conversational query than you're used to seeing. That's a reasonable sign it came through AI Mode.
  2. Review your negative keyword list. It's the main lever you have if a new placement is sending in traffic that doesn't fit what you sell.
  3. If you're running Shopping or Performance Max, check your Merchant Center feed. Google specifically recommends keeping product data complete, accurate, and current for this kind of placement, more so than for a standard search ad.
  4. Confirm your conversion tracking is solid before you draw conclusions from any shift in performance. Google recommends conversion- or value-based Smart Bidding backed by accurate tracking, and that advice means more when a new, less-familiar placement enters the mix.

None of this requires new campaigns, new budgets, or a new strategy. It requires knowing the ground shifted slightly under an account you already have.

What independent research is already finding

Google isn't the only source of data here. Productrise, a service that monitors product visibility in Google Shopping, tracked more than 100,000 identical shopping searches, side by side on standard Google search and AI Mode, for 21 days in July 2026 (US and UK only). It found products appeared on about 88% of standard search results pages versus about 23% in AI Mode, and when products did appear, standard search showed roughly 22.5 per page versus about 4.3 in AI Mode, a combined drop the study put at around 95%.

Treat that as a genuinely useful early signal, not a settled fact. Productrise sells monitoring tools built around this exact problem, which doesn't make its numbers wrong, but is worth knowing. The sample only covers two countries and short keyword-style searches rather than longer conversational ones, and Google hasn't publicly responded to the figures. The practical read for a Shopping or Performance Max advertiser: fewer of your products may be getting shown per AI Mode session than you'd expect from how your account performs in standard search, which is one more reason the account-check list above is worth five minutes of your time rather than an assumption that nothing changed.

What not to overreact to

Don't assume a sudden change in cost or conversions this week is automatically caused by AI Mode. Google's own internal CTR comparison is from a two-week window in August and September, in the US only, and it measured product-list ads specifically, not every ad format. That's a useful directional signal, not proof of what will happen in your account, your industry, or your market.

Don't rebuild your account around this either. The eligible campaign types, bidding, and billing are the same ones you're already running. This is a new place your existing setup can show up, not a new thing you need to build.

What's still uncertain

Google hasn't published a single definitive list of exactly which countries have ads in AI Mode live today versus planned for later this quarter, and its own two sources (the announcement and the Ads Help page) don't fully match on that list as of this writing. Google also hasn't published a breakdown of how much of a typical account's traffic is currently coming through AI Mode versus standard search, so there's no reliable way yet to estimate how much of your own budget this actually touches. Treat any specific percentage you see elsewhere online as an estimate, not a confirmed number from Google.

The bottom line

Nothing about this requires action, and nothing about this is optional. Your existing Search, Shopping, and Performance Max campaigns are already eligible for a newer, more conversational ad placement inside Google Search, and there's no setting that turns it off. The only real choice you have is whether you understand what's showing up in your reports, or you find out by accident three months from now when a number looks different and you don't know why.
