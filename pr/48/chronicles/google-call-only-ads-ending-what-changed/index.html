Google Is Shutting Off Call-Only Ads. The Deadline Is February 2027, But the Real Deadline Is Sooner.

Lonko Digital · 7 min read

If your Google Ads account runs call-only ads, the kind that only let someone call you and never click through to a website, Google has already started retiring them. The option to create new ones disappeared in early 2026. Every call-only ad still running today stops receiving impressions in February 2027. If that's the only ad in one of your ad groups, that ad group goes dark on that date, not just the ad.

That's a long runway, which is unusual for a Google Ads change. The risk with a long runway isn't that you won't hear about it in time. It's that a deadline over a year away doesn't feel like a deadline yet, and then it's suddenly three months out and your phone-lead ad groups haven't been touched since they were set up.

What a call-only ad actually is, and what replaces it

A call-only ad is its own ad format: it shows up in search results with a phone number and a "Call" button, and that's the entire point of the ad. There's no link to a website. Every result is either a call or nothing.

The replacement is not a new ad format. It's a responsive search ad, the same flexible ad type most Search campaigns already use, with a call asset attached. A call asset adds your phone number and a call button to a regular ad, so the same ad can generate either a call or a click to your website, whichever the person searching actually wants. Google isn't removing the ability to generate phone leads. It's removing the single-purpose ad format and folding call generation into the general-purpose one.

Your call assets themselves, the phone number extension, aren't going anywhere. If you're already running RSAs with call assets attached, this change doesn't affect you. The deadline is specifically about ad groups where a standalone call-only ad is still doing the work.

Check your account: how to find out if this affects you

Open the Ads page for a campaign (or go to the account-wide Ads & assets section) and look at the ad type for each ad. Call-only ads show up as their own distinct ad type, separate from responsive search ads or any text-based format. Two things are worth checking, not just one:

  1. Do you have any call-only ads at all? If you've built every campaign on responsive search ads, this change doesn't touch you directly, though it's still worth confirming your call assets are attached and current.
  2. For every call-only ad you find, is there anything else running in that same ad group? A call-only ad sitting alongside a responsive search ad in the same ad group is a lower-priority fix, since that ad group will keep serving the RSA after February 2027. A call-only ad that's the only ad in its ad group is the one that actually needs work before the deadline, because that ad group has nothing left to serve once the call-only ad stops.

If you work with an agency or freelancer on your account, this is a reasonable thing to ask them to check and report back on specifically, rather than assuming it's already been handled.

What to do now

For every ad group where a call-only ad is doing real work, whether or not it's the only ad there:

  1. Add your phone number as a call asset, at the account, campaign, or ad group level, if it isn't already. Call assets have to meet Google's call asset policy and be approved before they serve, so add the number early.
  2. Create (or edit) a responsive search ad in that ad group and attach the call asset to it. This gives the ad group a working replacement that can generate calls the same way the call-only ad did, while also picking up website clicks it couldn't generate before.
  3. Let the new RSA run alongside the old call-only ad for a while before deleting anything. Our advice: wait until the new ad is approved and has a few weeks of results before removing the old one. Don't delete the call-only ad the same day you add the RSA. Give it real time to show you how it performs.
  4. Keep call tracking turned on through the transition, specifically so you have a clean before-and-after comparison once the call-only ad is gone. This matters more than it sounds like it should, which is the next point.

The measurement point that matters

Here's the part that's easy to miss. A call-only ad can only ever produce one kind of result: a call. A responsive search ad with a call asset can produce two kinds: a call, or a website click. Once you make the switch, some of what used to show up as calls may start showing up as website clicks instead, from the exact same searchers who would have called before, and your account's own "calls" number can drop for reasons that have nothing to do with losing leads.

If you only watch calls, an RSA-with-call-asset migration can look like it's underperforming the old call-only ad even when total leads are flat or better. Count both calls and website conversions together when you judge how the new setup is doing, especially in the weeks right after a switch. Don't let a rise in website clicks hide a real drop in calls, and don't let a healthy number of website clicks get ignored because you're only watching the phone. If you're not already tracking which calls and clicks actually come from your ads, that gap tends to hide exactly this kind of shift (the real cost of not tracking conversions).

What we don't know

Google hasn't published any data comparing lead volume or lead quality between call-only ads and responsive search ads with call assets. Every source checked for this piece, Google's own and independent, recommends the switch and explains how to do it, but none of them say how performance actually compares once you've made it. If your results look different after switching, meaningfully better or meaningfully worse, that's genuinely useful information Google itself hasn't given advertisers in advance.

There's also a smaller, more specific gap worth naming plainly: Google's own sources don't fully agree on the exact month the option to create new call-only ads disappeared. Its developer-facing documentation says January 2026, tied to a confirmed API release on January 28, 2026, and states that this covered every interface including the regular Google Ads UI. Google's general advertiser-facing help pages say February 2026 for the same cutoff. Both are genuine Google sources; they simply don't match on a roughly four-week window. It doesn't change anything about what you need to do; creation is closed either way, and the date that actually affects your ad groups, February 2027, is consistent everywhere we checked. It's worth knowing the exact month isn't nailed down cleanly, rather than assuming more precision than actually exists.
